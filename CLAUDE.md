## Project Context

The patient wakes in a receiving room wearing a gown. Spectacles reveal a trapdoor; a wrench frees a key; a key opens the cabinet with the fungus; the fungus opens the floor. Below, a valve drains or floods the cistern, and past it a basin waits. Eight rooms on three levels, one path, one death, one ending. The horror is the prose changing under you: the same rooms described three ways as the player's perception moves from normal to clarity to corruption. The Inform 7 version lives in `C:/code/text-games/i7/feverdream/`; this is its Sharpee sibling.

## Design Documents

The story file is the design record. `legacy/src/` holds the TypeScript original (world, items, language layer, custom actions) and `legacy/tests/` its transcripts, kept for comparison until the Chord edition has shipped and been played.

## Writing Style Rules

- Clinical and flat in the normal state. Short declaratives.
- Clarity rewrites objects as anatomy without raising its voice: "They are not steel. They are cartilage."
- Corruption is the same sentences with letters swapped (3 for e, 0 for o, @ for a, 1 for i). Keep them readable; the point is that the player can still almost parse them.
- Room descriptions name their exits; this game predates the workspace style rule and keeps its Infocom feel.

## Key Design Decisions (do not change without discussion)

- Perception is the story's own state: `states: normal, clarity, corruption`, one-way. Eating the fungus moves it to clarity; the spray in the cistern moves it to corruption.
- Prose that the TypeScript rewrote in place is expressed two ways. Things the player examines carry `on the player examining` with one `phrase … when <state>` per state, which replaces the description. Rooms and scenery that only gain text carry `phrase detail while <state>:`, which appends to it.
- The spray fires one turn after the first entry to the cistern (`define sequence the spray exposure`), so the "chamber is alive" description is seen once before corruption takes it. The TypeScript fired it on entry and the clarity text was never shown.
- Story verbs (`breaking`, `draining`, `flooding`) dispatch through traits (`breakable`, `drainable`), not entity clauses; the loader refuses entity `on` clauses for story-defined actions. `turn valve` with no direction goes to the standard `turning` and prints the hint.
- The trapdoor, the glass case, and the brass key start out of play and are moved into the world when the spectacles are worn or the case breaks. Chord has no "concealed" flag.
- The cabinet needs `starts locked`; `lockable with` alone leaves a container unlocked.
- Transformed names from the TypeScript (crouching thing, bone shelves, arteries, organs, iron ribcage) survive as aliases, since Chord cannot rename an entity.
- Turning the valve right kills the player with `kill the player valve-right`; the basin wins with `win basin-touch`. No scoring.
